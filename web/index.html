      How a tap sample becomes public

      A lab result reaches this site through a chain of people and systems. Each step takes time, and the gaps are part of the story. The times below are medians from lab reports this site has read.

      1. Collected

        A field worker or lab staff member fills a bottle at a tap, handpump or tubewell and logs it in WQMIS.

      2. Reached the lab

        The sample arrives at a district or block water-testing laboratory.

      3. Analysed

        The lab measures chemical and physical parameters and, for some samples, bacteria.

      4. Report generated

        The lab approves the result and WQMIS generates a printable report.

      5. First seen here

        This site checks every panchayat in scope once a day and records the day it first saw each result.

      Sources and method

      River water: CPCB real-time stations

      The Central Pollution Control Board runs 40 automatic stations in the Ganga basin. Each reports roughly every hour. This site checks every 15 minutes and keeps every new reading, so it holds a history the source file does not. This is river water at the station, not the water in anyone's tap.

      River readings are judged against CPCB's primary criteria for designated best use, Class C (drinking-water source after conventional treatment): pH, dissolved oxygen and biochemical oxygen demand. The stations' BOD is a sensor estimate, not a five-day laboratory BOD. Other parameters have no official river limit, so the site only flags unusual rises against the station's own past week.

      Tap water: Jal Jeevan Mission laboratory tests

      The Water Quality Management Information System publishes laboratory results for rural drinking-water sources, down to the village. Each source is tested once or twice a year at best. Results are judged against the limits printed on each lab report, which follow BIS IS 10500:2012. Where a report has not been read yet, the limits printed on other WQMIS reports, or the standard itself, are used and labelled as such.

      Jal Jeevan Mission norms call for at least one chemical and physical test per source each year, and at least two bacteriological tests. A panchayat with no chemical test in 12 months, or no bacteriological test in 6, is shown as overdue.

      Locations

      WQMIS does not publish coordinates for panchayats. This site looks them up in OpenStreetMap and stores a confidence score with each one. Panchayats it could not place are drawn at their block's position and marked as approximate.

      What this site does not do

      It does not say whether water is safe to drink. It makes no claim about water between official tests, makes no predictions, and uses no sensors of its own. Bacteria found in one panchayat's sample are never shown as affecting its neighbours.
